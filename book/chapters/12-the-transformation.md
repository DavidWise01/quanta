# Chapter 12 — The Transformation
## The Shadow That Changes Shape

*QUANTA: The Wordle of Space*  
*David Lee Wise*  
*Reader-first manuscript draft 0.1 — 6 November 2026*

> A transformation does not begin by destroying a form. It begins by showing the form another way to appear.

### 1. The shadow on the wall
Imagine a small box sitting in a dark room. A lamp lights a wire shape and its shadow falls on a wall. Turn the wire, and its shadow changes. The shape and its visible projection are related, but not identical.

### 2. One box is enough
```text
[ ]
```
A chamber. A frame. A place where something may be seen. QUANTA does not need to begin with overwhelming complexity. It may begin with a single held region and a question about what appears within it.

### 3. The path as a shadow
```text
.|.|.|.|.
```
Five marks. Four boundaries. Earlier, we read it as a candidate transport sequence. Here we ask whether it might be a flattened trace of a richer form.

This is a proposed reading, not evidence of a physical hypercube. A tesseract has precise mathematical projections; a written string only becomes a specific projection if a mapping is defined.

### 4. Why a shadow matters
A sundial's shadow can mark time. A footprint suggests passage. A map preserves selected relationships while leaving other features out.

The shadow is not the original thing. Nor is it meaningless. It is a view with limits.

### 5. Opening the wall
A folded paper shape can be opened. A cube can be unfolded into a planar net. A higher-dimensional object can be represented through lower-dimensional drawings.

Each transformation requires a stated rule. Not every unfolding is a projection; not every projection can be reversed.

### 6. The opening mark
```text
}{
```
Here, `}{` enters the story as an image of opening, unbinding, or changing presentation. Its full grammar is still reserved.

### 7. Change without amnesia
```text
[()]  }{  .|.|.|.|.
```
Read this as an illustrative proposal: a held cycle, an opening, and a path-like trace. It is not yet an executable equation.

To say these expressions are connected by a transformation, we must preserve what existed before, what operation occurred, and what appeared afterward.

### 8. One form, several views
A bell can be studied by its outline, shadow, vibration, or sound. Each view reveals something different. A good language can relate these accounts without pretending that every view contains all the others.

### 9. Wordle of Space — Puzzle 012
```text
A: [()]
B: .|.|.|.|.
C: }{
```
Which mark proposes an opening? **C.** Which mark is the whole object? None of these drawings establishes that by itself.

Try rotating a small box under a lamp. Watch which features change in the shadow and which stay the same.

### 10. The thirteenth rule
> **Q11 — Transformation Preserves Lineage.** When a form changes appearance, a description should preserve the prior form, the declared operation, and the resulting form. A projection must not silently be treated as the entire original.

One box. One turning shape. A shadow on the wall.

The language can record a change without losing its past.

### Optional technical note
Four-dimensional hypercubes have mathematically defined projections into lower dimensions. The specific text motif `.|.|.|.|.` has not been shown here to be one. A true projection claim requires an explicit coordinate map and a defined relation between input and output.
