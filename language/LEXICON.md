## Introductory grammar subset (Chapter 10)

A dot is a mark. Matched `[ ]` and `( )` may nest. Crossed or unmatched delimiters fail the introductory check. Spaces are ignored. Other notation, including `|`, `{ }`, `}{`, and `{{i}}`, is reserved for later grammars and should not be silently interpreted as part of this subset.

## Three grammar families (Chapter 11)

- **Nesting:** paired square and round delimiters around dots, such as `[ ( . ) ]`.
- **Sequence:** alternating dots and bars, such as `.|.|.|.|.`. This is a teaching reading, not verified transport.
- **Identity reference:** the reserved whole token `{{i}}`. The reader recognizes it but does not invent execution semantics.

Composing these families into one executable sentence requires separately declared relationship operators.

## Use in the book

First show the mark; then give one sentence; then show one visual relation; finally provide optional formal detail.

Do not assert that the shape of a symbol proves the shape of a particle, or that a number represents a physical dimension without a testable definition.

## QG-044 — Closure order

- `.[(.])`: canonical **interleaved** box/fold closure (box closes before fold); one crossing of the written boundary intervals.
- `.[(.)]`: **nested** closure (fold closes before box); zero crossings.
- `.`: a vertex; `[ ]` and `( )` distinguish box and fold boundary types within this *new* dedicated grammar, which explicitly extends the earlier introductory chapter's nesting-only subset. Crossing written intervals does not establish 3D geometry.
- Invariant: `unfoldClosure(foldClosure(s)) === s`; preserve offsets, pairing and closure order, including spaces.

## QG-045 — Center-pinch geometry

- `n + .` (n = 3,4,5,8,13): planar *wheel graph* with n ring vertices, one center vertex, n perimeter edges, n radial edges. This is an explicit chosen geometric interpretation, not a universal reading of `|||` or `||||`.
- `pinch`: bounded displacement of the center and radial shortening of the ring; only accepted if every triangular spoke-face remains positively oriented and no disjoint edges cross at sampled checkpoints.
- `V=n+1, E=2n, F=n+1, V-E+F=2` for the planar embedded wheel including its exterior face. The graph's combinatorics are preserved by the operation.
- `1/1/2/3/5/8/13` are event checkpoints, not a Mandelbrot proof or physical capacitance law.

## QG-046 — First contact

- `s` is radial pinch strength; `r_i(s)=1-s*(0.55+0.45*cos(lobes*2*pi*i/n))`.
- `first contact` occurs at `s=1` for this declared wheel deformation because the index-zero radial factor reaches zero. This is a singular embedding, not a physical phase transition.

## QG-047 — Post-contact reconstruction

- `C+R0`: explicit successor vertex label after contracting the contacted `C--R0` spoke, not simply drawing overlapping points.
- `edgeLineage`: total mapping of source edges to successor edges or a documented deletion reason (self-loop/parallel deduplication).
- `V=n, E=2n-3, F=n-1`: contracted planar wheel counts including exterior face. Graph contraction changes the cell count while preserving Euler value 2; it is not evidence of a physical topological phase transition.

## QG-048 — Reversible pinch

- `freezePinch` stores full original vertices, original edges, and lineage alongside a contracted successor.
- `unfoldPinch` reconstructs the exact source and checks its independent deterministic contraction. The successor without preimage is insufficient for exact reversal.
- `unfold(fold(G))=G` is a reversible-data invariant only when source identity and edge evidence have been preserved; not a claim that physical pinching is reversible.

## QG-049 — Minimum fold memory

- `successor + recipe + lobes` is a smaller recipe-relative witness for the deterministic QG-047 source family, not an information-theoretically minimum description.
- `n` is inferred from the successor's vertex count; `lobes` declares the family member. Restored source is accepted only if regenerating the successor reproduces the provided successor exactly.
- A successor without the prior generative rule may admit multiple original graphs and cannot be assumed reversible.

## QG-050 — Ambiguous roots

- `root`: one candidate generating parameter in a fixed family, not a verified identity inferred from a silhouette.
- `observational equivalence`: two generators produce the same observations at every permitted sampled vertex. In the eight-vertex ring, cosine frequencies 1 and 7 are equivalent on the ring.
- `off-grid probe`: an additional observation chosen where candidate predictions differ, preserving the previous evidence and withholding identification until the candidates separate.

## QG-051 — Observation sufficiency

- `1 / 1`: two observation events at one permitted Fibonacci position, amplitude first and phase second, without moving to an unauthorized coordinate.
- `A_k(theta)`: amplitude channel `0.55+0.45*cos(k*theta)`. On the thirteen-position grid, roots 5/8 and 6/7 remain aliased for any number of amplitude-only measurements at grid vertices.
- `P_k(theta)`: phase-oriented channel `sin(k*theta)`. At vertex 1 it distinguishes the cosine-frequency aliases within the declared 8-root family.
- `sufficient`: exactly one candidate remains given the declared finite model and measured features; not proof of all possible physical roots.

## QG-052 — Independent information

- `information gain`: `log2(|compatible before|)-log2(|compatible after|)` under a uniform finite candidate prior; measured in model-relative bits.
- `repeated 1`: second permitted observation event at the same position; an amplitude duplicate may add zero information, while a phase measurement may distinguish ambiguous roots.
- `expected gain`: prior partition-based expectation across possible outcomes, distinct from realized gain after measurement.

## QG-053 — No-wasted-movement bound

- `stay`: take an unused informative measurement channel at the current allowed Fibonacci coordinate.
- `move`: advance only to the next permitted *different* Fibonacci coordinate if its expected gain, less a declared nonnegative movement penalty, is positive and best.
- `stop`: observed candidates are unique or no admissible action has strictly positive modeled net gain; uncertainty may remain.

## QG-054 — The cost of knowing

- `benefit`: expected candidate information bits multiplied by a declared, nonnegative value per bit.
- `cost`: declared per-channel observation credits at the **same** Fibonacci position 1; a separate budget constrains affordable measurements.
- `break-even`: `expectedBits*valuePerBit = sensorCost`; an observation is authorized only for strictly positive net benefit and adequate budget.

## QG-055 — The value of waiting

- `buy now`: an affordable current phase observation offers the strongest strictly positive expected net benefit.
- `wait`: an affordable later observation in a declared finite price schedule offers higher expected discounted net benefit; advance one simulation tick and recompute.
- `stop`: no feasible observation has strictly positive expected benefit after sensor cost and waiting fee. Remaining candidates are preserved rather than guessed.

## QG-056 — Uncertainty of waiting

- `forecast`: declared prices with normalized probabilities, not observed future facts.
- `realized price`: one branch disclosed after the observer chooses to wait; stored separately from the prior forecast.
- `wait option`: expected contingent surplus of buying only if useful and affordable after price is revealed, less the certain waiting fee.
- `stop after waiting`: retain ambiguity if the realized price does not justify observing phase; no fabricated evidence.

## QG-057 — Forecast reliability

- `issued forecast`: probability fixed in an event ledger before the corresponding binary price outcome becomes available.
- `Brier score`: squared difference between a predicted probability and a later observed binary outcome, aggregated by mean; lower is better on observed examples.
- `empirical rate`: observed event frequency, kept distinct from the smoothed posterior-predictive probability.
- `calibration bin`: grouping of forecasts by stated probability, showing observed frequency and sample count without claiming certainty from sparse evidence.

## QG-058 — Change versus noise

- `baseline`: the first fixed set of 20 binary observations, never retrospectively rewritten after monitoring begins.
- `monitor batch`: the next complete nonoverlapping set of 20 outcomes, tested only when complete.
- `alpha spending`: per-batch error allocation `alpha/[k(k+1)]` to bound the familywise false-alert rate under the stationary independent Bernoulli null hypothesis.
- `review`: a threshold exceedance signaling that the null model merits investigation, not independently proven world change.

## QG-059 — Sealed model handoff

- `sealedOld`: retained full source QG-058 change-alert ledger; immutable by the new operation, but not cryptographically protected against an external rewrite.
- `candidate`: alerting monitor batch interpreted provisionally, not automatically accepted as a new truth.
- `confirmation`: an independent new sequence of 20 observed outcomes, separate from baseline and seed batch.
- `promote / reject`: explicit finite-model decision after a complete confirmation batch; both preserve the old history and the fresh evidence.

## QG-060 — The right to be wrong

- `promotion history`: the QG-059 decision and its originating QG-058 alert, retained even when subsequent authority changes.
- `fresh validation`: a separate 20-outcome window following promotion, not a duplicated source or reused confirming batch.
- `withdrawn authority`: an explicit current status assigned when a fixed validation discrepancy threshold is exceeded; not deletion of previous evidence.
- `withdrawal certificate`: replayable record of promoted and validation rates, decision threshold, and old and new symbolic authority.

## QG-061 — Relearning without amnesia

- `ancestryRoot`: the asserted common origin of evidence packets; a GitHub/0root.ai mirror pair represents one claimed root, not two independent witnesses.
- `independent_claim`: the first packet attributed to a particular ancestry root; this is not external cryptographic verification of source independence.
- `mirrored_or_repeated`: later packet with the same root and outcome; retained but not double counted.
- `review_lineage`: contradictory outcomes attributed to one root, which block candidate promotion pending review.
- `eligible_for_provisional_authority`: three distinct root claims agree with the new candidate; a separate explicit action is still required.

## QG-062 — Three surfaces, independent roots

- `surface`: one of `github`, `0root.ai`, or `public-internet`; the first two are our owned information surfaces of one source ancestry.
- `connected ancestry component`: packets sharing any declared source or upstream ancestor, including links connected transitively; one component counts as at most one witness root.
- `robots policy`: declared model input `allow`, `disallow`, or `unknown`; not proof that a live site's actual rules were checked.
- `llms discovery`: `listed`, `unlisted`, or `unknown` metadata, never itself permission to access or disclose gated content.
- `review_shared_ancestry`: contradictory packet outcomes inside one traced evidence component; block authorization.

## QG-063 — Pinned source admission

- `pinned manifest`: pretrusted local reference mapping source + surface to a SHA-256 content digest, declared ancestry and modeled access status; the registry itself is not externally authenticated.
- `integrity match`: SHA-256 of the submitted text matches the pinned digest. This establishes consistency with the pinned copy, not actual authorship.
- `quarantine receipt`: retained, non-counting packet marked as unknown, digest mismatch, robots disallow or unknown access policy.
- `admitted evidence`: packet with a matched manifest and acceptable declared access status; only the manifest's ancestry enters QG-062's independent-root counter.

## QG-064 — The trust anchor

- `trust anchor`: independently supplied public Ed25519 verification key plus a pinned minimum accepted manifest revision; it is never taken from an untrusted evidence packet.
- `signed manifest`: canonical sorted-key source-registry payload and its Ed25519 signature, verified before QG-063 accepts packets.
- `rollback bound`: a signed but older revision is rejected if below the separately pinned minimum; without maintained external state a rollback cannot be detected.
- `authorship boundary`: a valid signature proves possession of a corresponding private key, not that the key holder owns the claimed publisher or that the associated evidence is true.

## QG-065 — Trust anchor rotation

- `dual-signed transition`: a canonical payload jointly signed by the current trusted Ed25519 key and proposed successor key.
- `key epoch`: immutable historical index binding a signed source manifest to the original verification key active when that manifest was recorded.
- `previousHash`: SHA-256 link to the preceding signed transition; verifies the locally supplied chain against insertion or alteration.
- `external rollback floor`: separately persisted minimum permitted sequence and source-manifest revision, required to reject otherwise valid old states.

## QG-066 — Recovery without the old key

- `recovery policy pin`: externally maintained allowlist of guardian public keys and their declared ancestral roots, plus historical QG-065 chain pin and rollback floors.
- `recovery petition`: canonical payload binding a specific historical chain digest, retired key fingerprint, successor public key and recovery sequence.
- `guardian quorum`: at least three unique pinned guardian signatures from three distinct policy-pinned ancestry roots, not three retellings of one approval.
- `successor proof of possession`: successor Ed25519 signature over the same petition, proving control of the candidate private key.
- `recovered epoch`: a separate signed-manifest context after recovery; old signed manifests remain verifiable under original historical key epochs.

## QG-067 — Revocation outlives recovery

- `historically valid signature`: a signature on original bytes verifying against the key active at its historical epoch; it does not confer current authority.
- `revocation certificate`: a successor-signed record binding the verified recovery digest, QG-065 historical digest, retired/successor fingerprints and explicit no-new-operations disposition for the old key.
- `current authorization gate`: checks revocation existence, unique operation sequence and signature under the new active key, never under a retired key.
- `revocation rollback floor`: externally maintained minimum revocation sequence; absent externally durable state the ledger alone cannot rule out valid older snapshots.

## QG-068 — Revocation across the hive

- `agent trust snapshot`: a complete local QG-067 signed-revocation and recovery history supplied by an individual synthetic agent.
- `externally pinned floor`: independent minimum revocation sequence, never derived from the untrusted agent snapshot.
- `stale`: previously internally valid but pre-revocation trust state; it cannot authorize new actions.
- `offline`: agent not able to attest current trust state at the authorization gate, denied despite locally cached signature validity.
- `current`: online snapshot cryptographically verified against an independent revocation floor; explicit authorization still required.

## QG-069 — Rejoining the hive

- `offline history`: append-by-value local observations preserved without gaining trust authority.
- `checkpointDigest`: a SHA-256 digest pinned independently of the rejoining agent; packet content cannot set its own expected digest.
- `verified-awaiting-authorization`: successful checkpoint and revocation replay does not itself authorize the agent to act.
- `rejoin certificate`: explicit modeled authorization bound to the verified checkpoint digest, agent ID and independently pinned revocation floor.
- `rejoined`: modeled locally verified authority, not real distributed consensus, remote identity proof or live permission grant.

## QG-070 — Freshness must be proven

- `issuer-signed challenge`: a fresh random 256-bit nonce plus agent identity, checkpoint digest, revocation floor, sequence and rejoin digest, authenticated by an independently pinned issuer public key.
- `agent challenge response`: signature from the pinned agent key over the exact issuer-signed challenge digest and current trust parameters.
- `consume-once`: value-appended challenge state transition that rejects duplicate response consumption in the modeled ledger; requires external durable state in a real deployment.
- `explicit fresh rejoin`: separate final approval after the challenge has verified and been consumed, preserving the original QG-069 offline observations.

## QG-071 — Authority has a lifetime

- `authority lease`: issuer-signed, finite logical-tick authorization bound to a verified QG-070 challenge, agent identity, checkpoint and revocation epoch.
- `exclusive expiry`: a lease authorizes only for `issuedAt <= tick < expiresAt` under a trusted externally supplied clock.
- `trusted clock floor`: independent monotonic minimum tick; a caller-provided modeled guard, not real-world secure time.
- `fresh renewal`: a new non-overlapping lease linked to its predecessor and a different verified QG-070 challenge; historical leases remain intact.
