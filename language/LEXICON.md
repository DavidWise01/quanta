## Introductory grammar subset (Chapter 10)

A dot is a mark. Matched `[ ]` and `( )` may nest. Crossed or unmatched delimiters fail the introductory check. Spaces are ignored. Other notation, including `|`, `{ }`, `}{`, and `{{i}}`, is reserved for later grammars and should not be silently interpreted as part of this subset.

## Three grammar families (Chapter 11)

- **Nesting:** paired square and round delimiters around dots, such as `[ ( . ) ]`.
- **Sequence:** alternating dots and bars, such as `.|.|.|.|.`. This is a teaching reading, not verified transport.
- **Identity reference:** the reserved whole token `{{i}}`. The reader recognizes it but does not invent execution semantics.

Composing these families into one executable sentence requires separately declared relationship operators.

## Use in the book

First show the mark; then give one sentence; then show one visual relation; finally provide optional formal detail.

Do not assert that the shape of a symbol proves the shape of a particle, or that a number represents a physical dimension without a testable definition.

## QG-044 — Closure order

- `.[(.])`: canonical **interleaved** box/fold closure (box closes before fold); one crossing of the written boundary intervals.
- `.[(.)]`: **nested** closure (fold closes before box); zero crossings.
- `.`: a vertex; `[ ]` and `( )` distinguish box and fold boundary types within this *new* dedicated grammar, which explicitly extends the earlier introductory chapter's nesting-only subset. Crossing written intervals does not establish 3D geometry.
- Invariant: `unfoldClosure(foldClosure(s)) === s`; preserve offsets, pairing and closure order, including spaces.

## QG-045 — Center-pinch geometry

- `n + .` (n = 3,4,5,8,13): planar *wheel graph* with n ring vertices, one center vertex, n perimeter edges, n radial edges. This is an explicit chosen geometric interpretation, not a universal reading of `|||` or `||||`.
- `pinch`: bounded displacement of the center and radial shortening of the ring; only accepted if every triangular spoke-face remains positively oriented and no disjoint edges cross at sampled checkpoints.
- `V=n+1, E=2n, F=n+1, V-E+F=2` for the planar embedded wheel including its exterior face. The graph's combinatorics are preserved by the operation.
- `1/1/2/3/5/8/13` are event checkpoints, not a Mandelbrot proof or physical capacitance law.

## QG-046 — First contact

- `s` is radial pinch strength; `r_i(s)=1-s*(0.55+0.45*cos(lobes*2*pi*i/n))`.
- `first contact` occurs at `s=1` for this declared wheel deformation because the index-zero radial factor reaches zero. This is a singular embedding, not a physical phase transition.

## QG-047 — Post-contact reconstruction

- `C+R0`: explicit successor vertex label after contracting the contacted `C--R0` spoke, not simply drawing overlapping points.
- `edgeLineage`: total mapping of source edges to successor edges or a documented deletion reason (self-loop/parallel deduplication).
- `V=n, E=2n-3, F=n-1`: contracted planar wheel counts including exterior face. Graph contraction changes the cell count while preserving Euler value 2; it is not evidence of a physical topological phase transition.
