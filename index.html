Book One · Origin

Chapter 1 — Zero

Imagine a page before its first mark.

Nothing has been drawn. Yet the page is not incapable of holding a drawing. A place can be chosen. A mark can be made. A relationship can begin.

QUANTA begins with a simple symbol:

0

This is our origin—the place we agree to begin reading. It is not a claim that nothing exists. It is a way of choosing where our story starts.

Then we make a mark.

.

A dot says: here.

Place it beside the origin and something changes. We can now speak about a position.

0  ····  .

Put down another dot and a new question appears: what is between them? That is the beginning of the gap.

.      .

It may be a measured distance, a space in a drawing, or a relationship we have not yet named. The answer depends on the world of rules we choose.

The first discovery

Move the label called zero. The dots do not have to move with it. Their relationship can remain exactly as it was.

We must learn the difference between changing what we call something and changing the thing itself.

The Wordle of Space · Puzzle 001

You have two dots. You redraw the origin somewhere else. Did the gap between the dots have to change?

Our first rule is a promise: preserve the relationship when only the label changes. We call it Q0 — Reference Preservation.

Next comes a dot that can be named, remembered, and compared to another dot.

Book One · Origin · Chapter 2

The Dot — The First Something

Imagine standing in a field at night. No roads. No buildings. No horizon you can recognize.

Then a single star appears.

.

You do not know how large it is or how far away it might be. But something has changed in your description of the world: there is a here.

The dot introduces distinction. It is a mark, not automatically a particle.

One dot becomes two

.       .

Two marks invite a comparison. Are they near? Far? Connected? The answer depends on the relationship we choose to describe.

Give the dots a name

{{i}}

A name gives us a way to follow an identity across descriptions. It does not make a mark conscious; it makes continuity something we can ask about.

The Gap — The Place Where Something Happens

Two marks. One space between them. What might happen there?

.    .

The gap is not simply a blank. It is the between: the place where a relationship can be measured, crossed, changed, or remembered.

Book One · Origin · Chapter 4

The Boundary — Where Inside Meets Outside

A child draws a line in the sand.

|

The ocean has not stopped. No wall has risen. Yet the drawing now has two sides.

A boundary can separate, guide, measure, filter, or permit passage. The boundary does not have to be a prison.

The chamber meets its doorway

[()]

Return to our engine: it needs a chamber to hold pressure, but also room for movement. The question is no longer merely whether something is inside or outside. It is what can pass, what can stay, and what can change?

Q3 — Boundary With Passage. If we claim an object crosses or is held by a boundary, we must say what allows that crossing or retention.

Book One · Origin · Chapter 5

The Box — The First Place That Can Hold Something

A child scoops water from a stream. Between their fingers, the water slips away. They try again with a cup. The water stays.

The water has not changed. The shape of the space has.

[ . ]

The dot is inside a place that can be named and revisited. The box is not the dot, and the dot is not the box.

Places within places

One region may belong inside another. A new level does not erase the distinction between what holds and what is held.
