## Proof

Let `R=[r18 r19 r20]` be a 4×3 integral rank-three matrix. Assume `r_i.T F4 r_i=-2` and `r_j.T H4 r_i=0` for each `i<j` among 18,19,20.

Set `W3=R.T H4 R`. Since `2r_i.T H4 r_i = -r_i.T F4 r_i=2`, the diagonal of W3 is (1,1,1). Its lower triangle is zero by assumption. Consequently, `W3` is upper unitriangular, and **det W3 = 1**.

**Automatic primitivity.** Let `E` be an integral basis for the saturation of the rank-three sublattice generated by R. Then `R=E D` for a nonsingular integral 3×3 matrix D. We have `det W3 = (det D)^2 det(E.T H4 E) = 1`; both determinants are integers, forcing `det D = +/-1`. Thus the three-root lattice is already primitive. It can be extended to a unimodular integer basis `P=[R,e]`.

**Automatic extension.** As `W3` is unimodular, choose an integer vector `a` so that `v=e+R a` satisfies `v.T H4 R=0`:
```text
a.T = -(e.T H4 R) * W3^{-1}.
```
All terms on the right are integral. The resulting `P'=[R,v]` remains unimodular. Then
```text
P'.T H4 P' = [ W3  * ]
             [  0  η ]
```
has determinant `det(P')² det(H4)=1`. Since det(W3)=1, **η=v.T H4 v=1**. Hence `v.T F4 v = -2η = -2`.

**Signed cofactor identity.** Let `C=R.T H4.T` (three rows, four columns) and define
```text
v_j = (-1)^j det(C with column j deleted), j=0,1,2,3.
```
Then `Cv=0`. The previous paragraph constructs a primitive integer generator of that rank-one kernel and shows it completes R unimodularly. Because H4 is unimodular and R primitive, the cofactor vector itself is that generator up to sign. Therefore its two signs **both** have norm -2 and yield unimodular determinant ±1, and both satisfy the three remaining Wall pairings with the preceding roots.
